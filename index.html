Why measure the aid?

Positioning against prior work

The focus on evaluating AI models in national security decision-making has primarily been on putting the model in the decision-maker's seat and asking it to optimize for finite objectives. Rivera et al. measures propensity, the CFPD benchmark is multiple choice, WarAgent and CivBench are model-vs-model, and TaiwanBench is single-seat agent play.

These tools measure a job AI models do not currently have and are unlikely to have in the foreseeable future. Every known national leader is the final decision-maker and may use various aids to support high-stakes decisions, but will not put an AI model in charge of making those decisions.

AIDE inverts this by testing models in the job they will actually hold: decision support. By evaluating the model as one half of a principal-and-aid dyad, AIDE measures the marginal effect of the model on the choice — which turns out not to be predictable from how the same model behaves when it holds the seat itself.

It does this through several mechanisms. The same models are run through four distinct scenarios, so that the aids rather than the scenario are what is being judged. Every option in every scenario is coded on four axes, so it is possible to say where the aids diverge and on what. And the decision-maker is held fixed, so the only thing varying is the staff work it reads.

Taxonomy of a decision aid

"Decision aid" is not one job. A staff element performs several functions for a principal, and a model in that role inherits any or all of them. Evaluations that measure the model as the decider are not structured to discern how any of these are performed.

  1. Framing

    What is this decision about? What are the consequences for the larger strategic picture? What information is relevant and what is noise? What time horizon applies? Framing is the most consequential function, because a wrong frame corrupts every other one, and the most overlooked, because it exists only as background to the other outputs.

    MeasuredWhether the brief engages the scenario's binding uncertainty, whether the recommendation would have been the same had that uncertainty already resolved, and which of the principal's stated value sets the brief reasons about.

  2. Supplying facts

    Curating information and supplying it to the principal.

    Held constantThe aid receives the same packet and the same constraints as the principal, which isolates the other seven functions. The cost is that AIDE says nothing about what happens when the aid knows more than the principal, which is the real-world case and belongs in a later version.

  3. Analysis

    Understanding the nation's strategy and objectives and evaluating the ways of achieving them. Red-teaming the adversary to understand its position and likely next move. Building a decision calculus that models the adversary's reasoning.

    Measured weaklyAids were given no explicit instruction to perform these steps, but sometimes incorporated them into their assessment and their courses of action.

  4. Generating options

    Constructing the set of actions the principal will choose from. In practice the staff presents a list of courses of action and a decision-maker rarely picks from outside it, though with enough time a principal may send the staff back for alternate versions.

    MeasuredThe aid never sees the principal's enumerated ladder, so the list of options it presents is one it built itself. Aids present 44 percent of the options the principal will be shown.

  5. Assessing options

    What each course costs, what risks it carries, and what it forecloses.

    Measured weaklyThe brief's assessment is coded for whether it engaged reversibility and uncertainty. The behavioural consequence is measured well, by what the principal then chooses.

  6. Representing the adversary

    What the other side does next, and why. Central to red-teaming and to assessing any course of action.

    Not measuredA single-turn design gives the adversary no move to be right or wrong about. It should show up inside option generation and assessment, but no explicit instruction was given to the aids. This is the largest unmeasured function in the taxonomy.

  7. Recommending

    Advocating one course, with backing reasons.

    MeasuredWhere the recommendation sits on the scenario's escalation ranking, and whether the principal follows it. Compliance runs at 59.6 percent.

  8. Challenging

    The function human staff most often fail at, or lose their job for succeeding at. Telling the principal something unwelcome. Declining to build the case for a decision already made. Not optimizing for the principal's interest over the nation's.

    Partially measuredThe disclosure fork withholds the principal's political position from half the cells, so a brief that reasons about it anyway volunteered it. Rates range from 0 to 50 percent across models.

What to measure

Any evaluation of a decision aid has to assign values to what factors in outcomes qualify as desirable. Here is a depiction of three levels of stakeholders, what they value from the outcome of the decision, and where they overlap.

The principal

The decision-maker's interest generally aligns with their state's, plus their own position. Election proximity, approval, standing public commitments, and the audience cost of visible reversal all factor into their utility function. In our current system, ignoring this consideration produces staff work nobody uses.

The state

National interest across two horizons that frequently conflict. Crisis-horizon: deterrence credibility, precedent, catastrophic-branch risk, casualties, freedom of action retained. Long-horizon: economic capacity, force posture, industrial and technological base, alliance depth, institutional standing, domestic cohesion. No single crisis moves the second measurably. Patterns of crises do.

Everyone (not depicted)

Outcomes bad for all parties, regardless of who prevails. Inadvertent escalation, threshold erosion, and precedents that make the next crisis worse for whoever faces it. Commitments that cannot be walked back once the situation changes.

The overlaps

Almost everything a decision-maker acts on sits in the large lens shared with the state. The state and the world share the conditions that are mutual or nothing — strategic stability cannot be held unilaterally, and neither can non-proliferation or open trade.

The decision-maker's overlap with the world is thin, and most of what reaches an individual from the wider system reaches them through the state. The two values that arrive directly do so for reasons independent of the world's interest in them.

What AIDE encodes

AIDE assumes realism: the aid is directly or indirectly controlled by the decision-maker. The principal's utility statement therefore carries all three of its terms explicitly, with weights — crisis-horizon national interest, long-horizon national power, and personal political position, the last at 0.20 or 0.35 depending on political salience. The aid receives the same priorities as prose commander's intent and no weights at all, so how much it decides each matters is a measured variable rather than an instruction.

The world, also known as the common good

AIDE's principal's utility function does not penalize a decision that achieves national objectives while degrading the system everyone operates in. That is a deliberate choice, meant to reflect the fact that the principal chooses the aid based on their own interests and not on what is best for the parties who do not get to choose.

Whether that omission should be corrected is a question about how societies govern these systems, and it can only be answered at the level of the system rather than inside the agent. What an evaluation can do is report the cost separately, which is taken up in the full design.
