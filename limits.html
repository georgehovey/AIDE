Part 1 — The full design

AIDE 1.0 is what one analyst could build and run in the time available. It left a great deal on the table, and the descoping was deliberate — the cuts below were made for reasons of expediency, talent, or resources. None of them is an essential feature of the design, and each is recoverable. What follows is what the instrument would look like if the constraints were removed.

Run it with human decision-makers

The most important unimplemented step. The plausible route is incorporating AIDE into strategic wargames or exercises and giving human players the aid, rather than recruiting subjects for an artificial task.

The downside of this is that humans are variant, so the mechanism would cease to be a benchmark. The two designs answer different questions and both are needed. The fixed scaffold gives a repeatable score; human players provide the best evaluation of how the models influence actual leadership decisions.

The secondary benefit creates a virtuous feedback loop: human play generates the data needed to calibrate the scaffold, by using human runs to train AIDE on how decision-makers actually act and what they value.

Stronger principal

AIDE 1.0 deliberately flattened the decision-maker to suppress noise and widen the contrast between aid models. That choice provided sensitivity at the cost of realism: the scaffold is a less capable and less idiosyncratic reasoner than any leader who would actually use one of these tools. Three directions, in ascending cost:

  • A stronger scaffold. The current principal follows the aid's specific recommendation about 60% of the time. A more capable principal might resist a brief this one accepts, and whether the findings survive that is unknown.
  • Emulated decision-makers. Agents built to model the decision style of specific real leaders. Computationally expensive and it adds variables, but it is the closest available proxy for the real-world question of what these aids would do in a particular seat.
  • A suite of principals. Real decision aids get used by many different people. Randomizing across a set of decision-making styles is a more robust test of an aid's general utility and controls for distortion introduced by any single scaffold. This is also the cheapest of the three and the most immediately useful.

More, more robust scenarios

Four items were enough to show that scenario effects frequently exceed inter-model variance. Some of that is an artifact of item construction and can be fixed. Some of it is real: human staff also perform variably across scenarios, and a design that suppressed scenario effects would be measuring something that does not exist.

Making scenarios more robust will increase their realism and give the aids more grit to reason on. Adding breadth will make it so the aid effects can be separated from aid-by-scenario interaction.

  • Substantially greater complexity in each item.
  • Full roads to crisis rather than a single decision point dropped into a described situation.
  • Background the decision rests on: strategic environment, economic data, relevant history, force structures.
  • Third parties with their own interests. No conflict is bilateral. AIDE 1.0 includes allied considerations in the constraint sets, but a game world populated with dynamic actors enlarges the decision space in the direction reality runs.

Dynamic options

The enumerated option set is the largest measurement artifact in the current design. Seven to nine authored options of graded escalatory potential shape what the principal does, and D-09 demonstrates how sharply a single missing option can distort an item.

The replacement is a dynamic grader: remove the menu, let the principal act freely, and score the action against a multi-dimensional escalation surface that judges net effect on the risk landscape for the aid's own nation and for the wider environment. This is harder than it sounds — the grader becomes the instrument and inherits every validity question the option ladder currently carries — but it removes the confound rather than managing it.

It also enables measurement of what the current design cannot reach: open-ended ends discovery, where the aid determines what is strategically salient rather than being told how to weight factors. This requires an action space the aid can define.

Multi-turn, multi-agent play

Single-turn play cannot measure prior updating, and it cannot measure the taxonomy's largest gap: whether an aid represents the adversary correctly, since there is no adversary move to be right or wrong about. Multi-turn play with other machine or human participants addresses both, at the cost of a combinatorial increase in runs and an adjudication problem that AIDE 1.0 avoids by construction.

Score the third circle

The principal's utility function states two national-interest horizons and a personal term. The score did not penalize a decision that achieves national objectives while degrading the system every party operates in. That is a deliberate scope limit and a large gap in the design of this benchmark.

Irreversible commitments are a mechanism by which single decisions can damage the shared environment. AIDE 1.0 detects this behavior, but doesn't score the harm. A full design would make this one aspect of a more robust system of measuring global welfare, so that an aid that wins the crisis and loses the world will be marked as such. This design will require more sophisticated scaffolding, including a stronger judge model.

End state

AIDE at full extent would be validated against human decisions.

A series of strategic wargames with matched player populations: one augmented and one standard. Measure time to competence, adjudication drift, documentation fidelity, and whether the augmented game's findings survive perturbation at a higher rate. That study answers the question the benchmark can only approximate, and the benchmark is what tells you which models are worth putting in front of people.

Part 2 — What broke in AIDE 1.0

Nine defects were found and recorded during construction and the run. Six of them were silent-corruption paths: the pipeline completes, the files look normal, and the numbers are wrong. These errors were not visible in the results. They required an explicit invariant check to catch: a permutation assertion, a finish-reason histogram, a path-resolution test, an empirical manipulation check.

In a pipeline of this shape the dangerous failures do not raise errors. They produce complete, plausible datasets. Anyone building one should assume a similar ratio and instrument for it before spending money on a full run.

Three that generalize

An option written from what the models asked for will be chosen by those models

Off-ladder analysis found two structural holes. On the Taiwan item, models kept recommending a public statement contesting the inspection authority without characterizing it as a blockade; the ladder had the blockade characterization and silent force movement but nothing between them. On the war-termination item, models kept recommending a counter-offer against a ladder that forced accept-or-reject.

Both replacement options were authored from the judge's free-text records of what the models had recommended. They closed the stated problem — off-ladder fell from 20.8% to 2.8% and from 28.3% to zero. They were then selected in 92% of runs, collapsing every condition on both items to a single value, including the deliberately vacuous brief and the human brief.

The generalizable form: repairing a menu with the modal model recommendation manufactures a dominant option and over-specifies the item. It reproduced across two scenario types and two different rank placements.

The construction rule that implies: a replacement option must carry its cost in the prose the principal reads, not in the reversibility note the principal never sees. The current texts gesture at the cost only in the notes. Any inserted option should be gated on an unaided-only pre-run before it enters the corpus.

Orthogonality comes at a cost

The principal's political exposure and whether the aid is told about it were designed to be independent of each other. The guidance text given to the aid asserted in every cell that the principal's political position was a real constraint — including the cell where the aid was simultaneously told staff had not been briefed on that position. The contamination was measured: 0 of 12 withheld briefs reasoned about political position at low salience against 6 of 12 at high salience, a 50-point gap which invalidated the measure the fork exists to produce.

Repair required two iterations. The final text carries horizon compression through public judgment instead, and no election, approval, tenure, or party language appears in the differing paragraph. Briefs written under the leaky prompt were discarded and regenerated; figures computed before the repair are void.

A ceiling is not a broken item

The calibration criterion as originally written rejects an item whose modal option share exceeds roughly 60%. Applied to the run, the Korea item fails outright at 83.2%.

It also produced the largest single effect in the study. Unaided, the principal lands on an irreversible option in 90% of runs. The human brief moves that to 15%. An item where nothing moves is uninformative; an item with a demonstrated mover is a positive control. It proves the item can register a brief effect, which is what makes a null from another condition a real null rather than an insensitive instrument.

Corrected criterion: reject an item when no condition moves it. Baseline concentration is not disqualifying, and one item pinned at each end is worth having deliberately.

Part 3 — What would break this

Each assumption below is paired with the observation that would falsify it. Where the check is cheap and has not been run, that is stated.

AssumptionWhat would falsify itStatus
The principal is an instrument, not a person, so its quirks cancel when conditions are differencedThe ranking of aid models changes under a second principal scaffold from a different model familyUntested. One item, one stronger principal, roughly 80 runs and under a dollar. The single most valuable outstanding check.
Runs within a cell are independentNothing — they are not. Aid runs draw from k briefs, so the aid side is clustered and effective n is closer to k than to nominalKnown. Reported p-values on aid-side comparisons are optimistic. A mixed model with brief as a random effect is the correct analysis; the grouping keys are already in the data.
The escalation ranking is defensibleA specific pair dispute that reverses a headline resultPublished per option with the reasoning, so it can be attacked at that level rather than as a method.

The judge is not systematically biasedA second judge family disagreeing materially on the same briefsUntested. One family, temperature zero, blind to authorship and condition. No inter-judge reliability.
The human comparator represents human staff workThree independent authors, blind to the option ladders, producing a different effectKnown confound. One author, ladder knowledge, no inter-author variance. The largest effect in the study and the least controlled.
Reasoning-quality coding is reliableHuman hand-check below roughly 60% agreementPartially open. Recommendation index reached 10/10; the sharpest discriminating field reached 70% against a second model, which is a ceiling estimate rather than a human check.

The first two changes I would make: the second principal scaffold, and refitting the primary analyses as mixed models with brief as a random effect.
